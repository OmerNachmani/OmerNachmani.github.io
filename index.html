About me

I am a system validation engineer at Intel and a final-year B.Sc. Electrical & Electronics Engineering student at Shenkar. My work has centered on system-level validation and automation, including firmware and driver debugging, Wi-Fi behavior, telemetry and log analysis, and Python/SQL-based engineering tools.

I am increasingly focused on security research — especially hardware security, side-channel analysis, reverse engineering, firmware, and the interfaces between software and silicon. I use personal and academic projects to go deeper into those areas and to build things I can test on real hardware.

Selected Projects

A few projects that best represent the direction I am building toward.

Hardware SecurityFPGAIn Progress

Side-Channel Analysis on a MIPS FPGA Core

Final-year research project implementing a pipelined MIPS core on an Artix-7 FPGA and evaluating power side-channel leakage with ChipWhisperer. The project covers leakage assessment and attacks using TVLA, CPA/DPA, followed by evaluation of countermeasures.

Stack: Verilog, Vivado, ChipWhisperer, Python, FPGA, MIPS

SecurityArduinoPython

SimpleTimingSCA

A hands-on timing side-channel demonstration against a PIN lock running on Arduino. A Python attacker measures response-time differences caused by an early-exit comparison and recovers the secret PIN one digit at a time.

PythonLogsAutomation

Totoro Log Scenario Finder

A desktop log-analysis utility that recursively scans large log sets against JSON-defined scenarios, supports regular expressions, and generates CSV reports. Built to turn repetitive log inspection into a reusable engineering workflow.
